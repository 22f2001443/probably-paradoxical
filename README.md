# Probably Paradoxical

Probably Paradoxical is the public portal for a team-based statistical paradox
exploration competition hosted as part of Paradox'26 at IIT Madras.

The event invites teams to choose a statistical paradox, design a study around
it, collect real-world responses, analyse the data, and present what they find.
The portal is the central place to understand the event, follow updates, read
guidelines, see the timeline, and access team-related pages.

## What The Event Is About

Probably Paradoxical is built around the idea that data can surprise us. A trend
that looks obvious in one view can reverse, disappear, or become more complex
when the same data is grouped differently.

Teams explore this idea through a complete mini research cycle:

1. Learn about statistical paradoxes and how they appear in real data.
2. Pick a paradox or theme to investigate.
3. Design a questionnaire or study plan.
4. Collect responses from the target population.
5. Clean and analyse the collected data.
6. Present the findings to judges and guests.

The goal is not only to get a result, but to explain the reasoning clearly:
what was expected, what the data showed, and what the team learned from the
process.

## Event Stages

### Stage -1: Pre-event Expert Talk Session

An expert-led session introduces the ideas behind statistical paradoxes and
helps teams understand how to think about research framing, data, and
interpretation before the competition begins.

If a recording is available, the timeline will include a link to watch it.

### Stage 0: Problem Statement Release

The event brief and eligible paradoxes are shared. Teams study the problem
space and begin thinking about the kind of data they want to collect.

### Stage 1: Theme And Questionnaire Design

Teams choose their paradox or theme and prepare a questionnaire or study plan.
This stage is about clarity, relevance, and making sure the proposed data
collection can actually answer the team's question.

### Stage 2: Data Collection

Teams collect responses from the target population. The focus is on reaching
enough people, collecting data responsibly, and keeping records clean enough for
analysis.

### Stage 3: Data Cleaning And Analysis

Teams prepare the data for interpretation. This includes cleaning responses,
checking assumptions, looking for patterns, and deciding whether the paradox
appears in the collected data.

### Stage 4: Final Presentation

Teams present their findings to judges and guests. A good presentation explains
the question, the method, the data, the result, and the team's interpretation
without hiding uncertainty or limitations.

## Good Participation Habits

Keep your research question focused.

Design questions that people can answer clearly.

Collect data honestly and respectfully.

Document your assumptions and decisions as you go.

Do not treat unexpected results as failure. In this event, surprise is often
where the most interesting explanation begins.

Make your final story understandable to someone who has not followed your work
from the beginning.
